Getting ready can be a step toward greater independence.
“My child wants to come outside with us. How can putting on shoes become something they take part in?”
Illustrative process:
1. Start with what your child can do.
Describe how your child chooses shoes, sits, reaches or asks for help. The starting picture includes the support that makes the moment possible.
2. Choose an everyday priority.
Agree a meaningful part of getting ready. Communication, movement, sensory comfort or sequencing support follows that individual goal.
3. Make the idea work at home.
Ask for a manageable opportunity during a real outing routine, with clear guidance on what to offer and what to notice.
4. Review the help and participation.
Bring back what happened. Discuss what your child took part in, what help still mattered and which next step would be useful.

Use manageable, guided practice in meaningful routines. Family participation connects the work with daily life.

5. Track and correct
Bring relevant observations back into review. Ask what became useful, what support remains and what needs to change.

Compare abilities and context over time. Continue, adapt or complete support according to the child’s needs.

Keep self-sufficiency, learning, relationships and mainstream participation in view. Progress is individual.
