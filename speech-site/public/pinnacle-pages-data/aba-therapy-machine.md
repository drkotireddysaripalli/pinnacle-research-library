Pinnacle describes ABA and behavioural support as one possible part of a child-specific, family-guided plan. The page begins with the child’s everyday life: communicating needs, making choices, moving through routines, learning, playing and participating with appropriate support.

## Direct answer

Applied Behavior Analysis, also written Applied Behaviour Analysis, uses careful observation and learning principles to understand behaviour and support useful abilities. Meaningful goals should connect to the child’s communication, comfort, safety, participation and quality of life. Individual assessment and professional judgement guide whether behavioural support is appropriate.

## What assessment may consider

- what happened before, during and after the moment;
- communication and understanding;
- pain, sleep, health or emotional factors that may require appropriate review;
- sensory and physical environment;
- task demands, predictability and available choices;
- what the child already does successfully; and
- family, caregiver and teacher observations.

## Pinnacle's life-first direction

Pinnacle connects ability measurement, agreed goals, appropriate therapies and people, manageable everyday practice, observation, review and reassessment to a direction toward growing self-sufficiency and mainstream participation. This is a programme direction, not a guaranteed outcome.
