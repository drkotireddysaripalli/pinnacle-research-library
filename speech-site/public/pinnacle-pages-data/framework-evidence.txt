“I want my child to ask for help, join in and take more of a part in everyday life.” Start there. The framework gives assessment, relevant therapies, family practice and review a shared purpose that makes sense in your child’s day.

The Pinnacle Global Autism Framework is Pinnacle’s documented approach to organising child-development support around a self-sufficient, mainstream-included life. The purpose determines what abilities to understand, which goals and professional contributions matter, what practice fits everyday life and how observations shape the next review.

1. A life moment comes first — The family wants the child to take part in a classroom activity and ask for help when needed. Describe the child’s interests, communication and present support.

2. Understand what would help — A suitable professional considers the relevant abilities and context. With consent, useful teacher observations can add the classroom perspective.

3. Choose relevant people and methods — Agree a meaningful communication step, the support involved and a manageable opportunity to practise at home or in learning. No fixed therapy bundle is assumed.

4. Review usefulness in real life — Discuss whether the child could communicate a need, how comfortable participation felt, which help worked and what should change next.

Describe what you want to open up — Bring an everyday moment, an interest and the support your child already uses.

Understand and question the goal — Ask why a priority matters, how it serves your child’s life and what a useful next step could look like.

Make practice fit your day — Agree manageable, guided opportunities. Family participation is informed and supported, rather than another burden.

Help shape the next review — Bring your observations and the child’s response back to the professional. Keep useful support, adapt what needs to change and review again.

1. Understand abilities — Start with strengths, everyday observations and suitable assessment. Read AbilityScore with the child’s age, context and skills. https://www.pinnacleblooms.org/abilityscore

2. Plan for readiness — Discuss what you want to make possible and agree useful priorities. Readiness views support professional judgement. https://www.pinnacleblooms.org/seven-readiness-indexes

3. Choose relevant support — Bring in the therapies, educational support or health review that fit this child. Every child does not need every service. https://www.pinnacleblooms.org/autism-therapy

4. Practise in everyday life — Use manageable, guided practice in meaningful routines. Family participation connects the work with daily life. https://www.pinnacleblooms.org/everyday-therapy

5. Track and correct — Bring relevant observations back into review. Ask what became useful, what support remains and what needs to change. https://www.pinnacleblooms.org/fusion-module

6. Reassess and repeat — Compare abilities and context over time. Continue, adapt or complete support according to the child’s needs. https://www.pinnacleblooms.org/reassess-review-repeat

7. Grow independence and participation — Keep self-sufficiency, learning, relationships and mainstream participation in view. Progress is individual. https://www.pinnacleblooms.org/pinnacleai

What is the Pinnacle Global Autism Framework?
It is Pinnacle’s documented approach to organising child-development support around a self-sufficient, mainstream-included life. That purpose guides abilities, goals, relevant people and methods, everyday practice and review.

What is the starting point for a family?
Start with one meaningful everyday possibility, such as being understood, asking for help, joining an activity or taking part in a routine. Present abilities and context guide the next assessment and support discussion.

How do the therapies fit together?
Relevant speech, occupational, behavioural and educational contributions follow the child-specific priorities. The framework keeps the everyday life purpose in view; it does not assume every child needs every therapy.

How can parents shape the work?
Describe the child’s day, understand and question priorities, agree manageable guided practice and bring observations into professional review. The child’s response and useful support can change the next decision.
