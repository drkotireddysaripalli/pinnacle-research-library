Parent moment: “My child wants to join the group. Which support would actually help?”
Decision this page explains: Which of the seven views matters for this goal now, what was observed and what practical support should be tried?
Family discussion: A more specific discussion of the child’s strengths, context and next opportunity, with each view interpreted by the team.

Direct answer: The BIS record names School, Speech, Motor, Study IQ, Behavior, Self Sufficiency and Mainstream Inclusion Readiness Indexes. These are separate software views that support discussion and planning; they do not certify school entry, intelligence, social worth or future independence.
Life purpose: Readiness is useful when it points to a meaningful next opportunity in the child’s life and the support needed to try it.
Boundary: The names are those printed in the BIS schedule. They should not be read as a formal school admission decision or a promise of mainstream placement. Report versions have used different display scales; use the current report and professional explanation.

Illustrative example: A classroom goal has more than one doorway.
Illustrative example only. A child enjoys group play but needs help asking a peer to join.
1. Notice strengths: The child may already follow a routine and enjoy peers.
2. Look at relevant views: Communication and school participation might matter here; the other views remain context rather than automatic targets.
3. Choose suitable support: A professional and family agree on an accessible invitation method and what the school can do.
4. Review in real life: The team asks whether the child could join and enjoy play, not whether an index alone improved.
