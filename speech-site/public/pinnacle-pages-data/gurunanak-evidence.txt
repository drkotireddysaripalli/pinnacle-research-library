1. Notice the starting ability.
Share how your child recognises, chooses or handles a familiar item. Discuss what they do themselves and what help they use.
2. Agree one everyday priority.
Choose a meaningful part of getting ready. Relevant communication, movement, sequencing or learning support can follow that goal.
3. Make practice manageable.
Ask for a short, suitable routine with clear guidance. Family observations show whether it fits the real morning, not just the therapy room.
4. Review participation.
Discuss what became easier, which support still helps and whether the next step needs changing. Progress is individual.
