Direct answer: Pinnacle’s documented Everyday Therapy workflow links goal-specific activities to family practice and feedback. The source method describes a four-day look-ahead in one version; the actual plan and pace should be selected for the child rather than treated as a fixed daily quota.
Life purpose: Home is not a second clinic. The family helps a skill become useful in ordinary life without carrying the professional’s responsibility alone.
Boundary: The described programme does not mean all children receive the same number of techniques or that parents must deliver therapy themselves. No particular improvement or schedule is guaranteed.

Illustrative example: Getting ready to eat together.
Illustrative example only. A child wants to take part in a family meal but finds one step difficult.
1. Choose one moment: The family and professional agree on a small, worthwhile participation goal.
2. Demonstrate an approach: The professional explains an accessible cue, adaptation or activity and checks that it suits the child.
3. Try it in normal life: The family uses it briefly in a natural mealtime when appropriate; comfort and consent matter.
4. Bring back observations: What worked, what felt hard and what the child preferred guide the next review.
