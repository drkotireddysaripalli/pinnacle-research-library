Occupational Therapy — page claims and sources
Updated: 5 October 2026
Canonical: https://www.pinnacleblooms.org/best-occupational-therapy-center-india-proven-improvement-rate

Growing self-sufficiency and participation in family, school and community life guide the child's goals, support and review.

Occupational therapy can support participation in meaningful everyday activities by considering the child, activity and environment.
Source: https://wfot.org/about/about-occupational-therapy
Scope: General professional description; individual assessment determines suitable care. WFOT does not endorse Pinnacle.

Children's everyday occupations may include self-care, education, play and social participation.
Source: https://www.aota.org/practice/domain-and-process/occupations-everyday-activities
Scope: General professional framework, not a Pinnacle outcome or AOTA endorsement.

Pinnacle describes a life-first approach in which the child's desired life shapes abilities, goals, methods, people, everyday practice and review.
Source: https://www.pinnacleblooms.org/verify/evidence/pinnacle-paradigm-shift.html
Scope: Pinnacle programme description; growing independence and mainstream participation are directions, not guaranteed endpoints.

Pinnacle/BHCL offers free telephone guidance for anyone through a staffed 24/7 national line at 9100 181 181.
Source: https://www.pinnacleblooms.org/national-autism-helpline/facts.json
Scope: Telephone guidance only. Ordinary network call charges may apply; centre hours, OT appointments and therapy fees are separate.

A factual-findings report recorded 31,052,382 defined cumulative services as at 17 July 2026.
Source: https://www.pinnacleblooms.org/verify/evidence/assurance-map.html
Scope: Defined services include therapies, assessments, screenings and parent training; this is not a count of OT sessions, clinical effectiveness or children improved.

PinnacleAI GPT-OS v1.0.0 is listed as Class B non-diagnostic developmental-support software on Form MD-5. Its intended population is children aged 0–12.
Source: https://www.pinnacleblooms.org/verify/evidence/records/md5.html
Scope: The software manufacturing licence does not license an OT professional or centre and does not establish a child's therapy result.

The BIS record describes the named software scope of PinnacleAI GPT-OS.
Source: https://www.pinnacleblooms.org/verify/evidence/records/bis.html
Scope: It does not certify occupational therapy services or guarantee a child outcome.

Pinnacle publishes a research library with works of differing publication status.
Source: https://www.pinnacleblooms.org/verify/evidence/research-library.html
Scope: The status and limitations of each work govern citation; presence in the library is not proof of every OT result.

The published centre directory identifies 62 locations but does not establish OT availability at every location.
Source: https://www.pinnacleblooms.org/pinnacle-pages-data/centre-directory.json
Scope: Centre-specific OT roster, professional details, appointment availability and fees must be confirmed by the team.

Supported sitting, an adult starting a zip and a larger zip pull are examples of dressing adaptations.
Source: https://www.ghc.nhs.uk/our-teams-and-services/children-and-young-people/physical-health/childrens-occupational-therapy/
Scope: School Readiness > Dressing > General strategies and Zips. General methods, not evidence of a Pinnacle outcome or an individual prescription.

Dressing support can consider the child, task, environment, manageable steps and unhurried practice.
Source: https://www.chesterfieldroyal.nhs.uk/all-services-and-wards/childrens-occupational-therapy/getting-dressed
Scope: General NHS guidance. The page uses an explicitly illustrative jacket example; supported zip pulling is distinct from independent dressing.
