The game your child wants to join. The help they want to ask for. The small task they want to do themselves. We give each activity a reason in that life—and keep learning from your child’s response.

## What TherapeuticAI does
TherapeuticAI® is PinnacleAI’s intervention-support layer, connecting a professionally agreed goal with suitable guided activity and response review. The BIS scope schedule names Everyday Therapy through TherapeuticAI. The professional chooses and adapts the support with the family; the software does not independently prescribe treatment.

## Purpose from the beginning
Your child’s self-sufficient, mainstream-included life is the purpose from the beginning. TherapeuticAI® connects that direction with professionally chosen activities, family understanding and continuing review.

## One illustrative play-box decision
An illustrative example, not a patient case: a four-year-old chooses a toy from an open play box, enjoys playing beside a parent and sometimes brings an object to ask for help. The family wants that request to be easier for other familiar adults to understand.

## A request that lets play continue.
The child chooses a toy with a part they want help to use. The goal is an accessible way to ask for assistance during chosen play. Toys remain available; help is not withheld to demand a response.

## Make help easy to ask for.
After considering the child’s communication, the professional models an agreed help gesture alongside a simple picture cue. The adult can respond to the child’s existing way of asking as well as the new option. Spoken words are not a condition for help.

## The child shows a useful route.
In this fictional moment, the child brings the toy to the parent and places it in their open hand, without using the picture cue. The parent helps and play continues. That is an observed request by action; the unused picture alone does not explain why.

## The professional decision changes
The professional and family retain the child’s object-giving request, agree how another familiar adult will recognise and respond to it, and make the picture cue an available option rather than a requirement. The next opportunity is brief, child-chosen play with that familiar adult.

## The next observation
Describe how the child asked, who was present, what prompting or help was used and whether play continued comfortably. The next adult’s response and the child’s use across settings are still to be observed.

## Keep what helps the child take part
If the familiar adult recognises the request and the child chooses to continue, record the support still needed. The team can decide whether to repeat that opportunity or explore a small change. One example is not proof of independent use everywhere.

## Change what gets in the way
If the request is missed or the child moves away, offer help and respect the choice to stop. Review the cue, adult response, activity or setting. More repetitions of the same task are not automatically the right next step.

## Your family’s role
Know why this activity belongs. You should be able to connect the activity to something your child wants or needs to do in everyday life.
Know who is guiding the choice. Ask the professional to show the support, explain their role and agree what your family can comfortably try.
Know what to bring back. A useful observation includes the setting, the child’s response and the help involved—not only whether a task was completed.

## What is TherapeuticAI?
TherapeuticAI is PinnacleAI’s intervention-support layer. It connects a professionally agreed goal with suitable guided activity and response review. The BIS scope schedule names Everyday Therapy through TherapeuticAI.

## How does an activity connect to my child’s independence?
We begin with an everyday purpose, such as asking for help during chosen play. The professional selects a suitable activity and support, explains the connection to your family and reviews whether the skill becomes useful in that setting. Growing independence and participation guide the work.

## Who chooses the activity for my child?
A suitable professional interprets relevant assessment and context, discusses the family’s priorities and selects or adapts support. Software supports the plan; it does not independently prescribe treatment or replace professional judgement.

## Does every child need all four therapies?
No. Speech Therapy, Occupational Therapy, ABA Therapy and Special Education contribute where they are suitable for the child’s needs and goal. A diagnosis alone does not determine a fixed therapy combination.

## What if my child asks in a different way or does not want to continue?
The child’s existing communication, comfort and choice matter. In the illustrative play-box example, giving an object to an adult is recognised as a request even when the picture cue is unused. The professional can change the support or activity; a child can pause or decline.

## What does the family do between visits?
Use the manageable guidance agreed with the professional in a suitable everyday moment. Tell the team what your child chose, how they responded, what help was needed and whether the situation differed. You remain the parent or caregiver; the professional guides clinical decisions.

## What do the BIS and MD-5 records establish?
BIS documents the specified software quality-system scope and names Everyday Therapy through TherapeuticAI. MD-5 covers the named non-diagnostic Class B device and its intended use. They do not certify every therapist or guarantee that an activity will produce an individual outcome.
