Reassess, Review and Repeat | Keep the Child’s Plan Accountable

Parent moment: “What changed, and what still needs a better approach?”
Decision this page explains: How does today compare with the earlier observation, including setting and support, and which decision follows?
Family discussion: A reasoned review of progress, no change or a new need, with the next decision explained.

Direct answer: Pinnacle’s documented workflow describes periodic reassessment, updated AbilityScore and Readiness views, and plan correction. The methods preprint discusses monthly review in one programme; the right schedule for a child is a professional decision, not a universal promise.
Life purpose: Review asks whether a skill is becoming useful in life and whether the child’s comfort, goals and context still fit the plan.
Boundary: Repeated reassessment does not guarantee a child will become independent or receive school placement. A score change alone is not proof of a particular life outcome.

Illustrative example: The child can now pack a bag—with one important exception.
Illustrative example only. At home the child completes most steps, while a busy school arrival still needs support.
1. Compare fairly: Look at the same ability with the support and environment clearly described.
2. Listen to life: Family and teacher observations show where the skill transfers and where it does not.
3. Make a real decision: The team may keep a useful support, change a cue, address the setting or choose a new goal.
4. Check again: Later measurement and observation test whether that decision helped.
