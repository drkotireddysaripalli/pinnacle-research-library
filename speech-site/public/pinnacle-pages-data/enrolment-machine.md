## “I want my child to ask for water.”

An illustrative family conversation

**Notice what already works.**

In this example, the child points to a familiar cup when it is visible. When it is elsewhere, the parent has to guess what the child wants.

**Agree one useful adjustment.**

A speech and language professional could explore an accessible request—words, a gesture or a picture—with the child and family.

**Bring it into an ordinary moment.**

The parent offers water as usual and, with professional guidance, makes the agreed communication support available at mealtimes.

**Know what to review.**

At the next review, compare how the child requests water and what help is needed. Keep useful support or adjust it together.

This proposed adjustment has no observed result yet. Your child’s goal, communication method and support are agreed individually.

## A useful next step. A review you can understand.

In the water-request example, the question is what helps the child communicate a need in an ordinary moment. Your observations give the professional something specific to review—not just a count of sessions attended.

From guided work to everyday life. A developing ability becomes meaningful when the child can use it in the moments that matter.

### The request works with support

If the child uses the agreed request with an adult’s prompt, keep useful support and record the prompting still needed. That is not yet a request made without the prompt. A picture or communication aid can remain part of independent communication.

### The support is not accessible

If the picture, gesture or words do not help the child express a choice comfortably, the professional reviews that method and the setting with your family.

### It works in one setting

If a request is useful at the table, agree how to check it with another familiar person or in another suitable routine. Transfer is something to observe, not assume.

These are possible review decisions, not reported outcomes. Family observations and professional judgement determine the next step.

## Before your first visit

What makes the Pinnacle approach different?

We begin with the life your child is growing toward. Greater independence and participation in family, school and community give assessment, goals, therapies, everyday practice and review one shared purpose.

What can I discuss in the first conversation?

Tell us about everyday moments you want to make easier: communicating a need, managing a routine, learning or taking part with others. Ask about assessment, suitable support, the centre and fees. You do not need to know the name of a therapy to begin.

How will my family be involved?

Your priorities and observations help shape the work. Parent-guided everyday practice connects developing abilities with daily routines, and review helps the team decide what to adjust next.

What should I bring to a visit?

Bring your questions and any existing reports you would like the professional to consider. The team will confirm what is relevant for your appointment. There is no need to upload medical information to this enquiry form.

Do I need to choose a therapy before contacting you?

No. Select “Help me choose” or call us. An appropriate professional assessment helps determine what support is suitable.

What happens after I request a family conversation?

When the form confirms receipt, your enquiry has been accepted. Our team contacts you to discuss your priorities, a suitable centre and professional, appointment options and fees. Submitting the form does not itself confirm an appointment or enrol your child.

Can I choose a centre and see its details?

Yes. Choose a preferred centre to see its address and available photos, contact details and Google Maps link. You can also leave the choice open. We confirm current premises, suitable services and appointment availability before you travel.

What should I do if the form cannot confirm receipt?

Your details stay in the form. If receipt is uncertain, call 9100 181 181 before sending again so the team can help you check. If the form explicitly rejects the request, check the details or call us.

Is the assessment free?

The current speech and language assessment campaign offers a FREE assessment. Other assessments and ongoing therapy may have different fees. Confirm the service and its charges with the team before your visit.
