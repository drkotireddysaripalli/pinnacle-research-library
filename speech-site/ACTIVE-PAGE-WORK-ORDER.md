## Narrative contract

1. Begin with communication, choice, routines and participation rather than obedience or a diagnostic label.
2. Explain ABA in plain language and distinguish an educational definition from endorsement or staff credentialing.
3. Invite one recognisable everyday moment, then examine communication, health, sensory and environmental context before describing support.
4. Connect every goal to the child's wider life through Pinnacle's seven-stage life-first pathway.
5. Use a respectful transition example: understand the moment, support a way to communicate “break/help/not yet”, carry it into daily life and review burden and participation.
6. Show distinct contributions from behavioural support, speech, occupational therapy, special education, family/school and relevant health professionals.
7. Explain PinnacleAI's non-diagnostic measurement and review role within the exact MD-5 and BIS scope.
8. Place family knowledge and the child's communication, comfort, assent and preferences inside every review.
9. Present licences, research, recognition and government source letters through Verify with their exact status and limitations.

## Claim boundaries

- Do not promise cure, compliance, normalization, independence, school entry, mainstream inclusion or a particular result.
- Do not present forced eye contact, suppressing harmless differences or compliance for its own sake as a goal.
- Do not claim ABA at every centre, universal staff credentials, fixed intensity, named assessment tools, home/school delivery, FREE ABA assessment, rating or review volume without current operational evidence.
- Do not use world-first/only/#1, unqualified improvement percentages, granted-patent language or licences as outcome proof.
- PinnacleAI GPT-OS v1.0.0 is non-diagnostic developmental-support Class B SaMD. The software scope is separate from therapy effectiveness and centre registration.
- External NICE, AAP, WHO and BACB references do not endorse Pinnacle.
- Government letters and awards retain their stated context and stage; they are secondary evidence, not clinical endorsement.
