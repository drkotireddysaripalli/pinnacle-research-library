# Occupational Therapy visual and narrative revision

## Page promise

Help a family see how an everyday activity can become a practical goal, how the child and environment are assessed, how suitable occupational therapy and family-guided practice connect, and how real participation at home, play and school is reviewed. Growing self-sufficiency and mainstream participation give direction; no individual outcome is promised.

## Image 1 — life in view

Create a 16:9 page visual, no embedded words, logos, numbers, certificates or seals. In one luminous white field, show a young Indian child at three connected everyday moments: at home fastening their own schoolbag while mother supports without taking over; at play using both hands to join another child; at school entering an ordinary learning activity. Keep the same child recognisable across all moments, but avoid hard triptych boxes or before/after comparison. Use three organic soft-edge photographic vignettes linked by a vivid flowing pathway in teal, purple, cyan, yellow and green. The home moment is dominant and richly photographic; other moments are smaller and aspirational. Include the official Pinnacle Blooms Network multicolour emblem only if faithfully rendered from the supplied logo reference; otherwise leave it out, since the adjacent HTML carries the real brand. The family should look capable and hopeful, not distressed. No uniforms implying a guaranteed school place. This is an illustrative journey, not an actual case or treatment result. Make it visually excellent on 390 px mobile cropping as well as desktop. Preserve generous open white space to let the neighbouring HTML copy breathe.

## Image 2 — first conversation

Create a warm 3:2 editorial visual, no embedded text. An Indian mother and a child around five sit at a low table with a Pinnacle occupational-therapy professional. The professional wears a full-sleeve white professional coat with the Pinnacle multicolour emblem and small readable Pinnacle Blooms Network name on the chest; no stethoscope or doctor identity. The child chooses a familiar daily activity from real objects: a jacket fastener, spoon and small schoolbag. The professional listens to the mother and observes the child rather than testing the child mechanically. Compose faces and hands naturally, bright white environment with a soft teal-and-coral organic border and subtle violet/cyan pathway hint, no clinical dashboard or invented credential frame. A calm, collaborative moment that makes the first call feel worthwhile. No actual patient or staff representation, testimonial, review or guaranteed outcome.
