# Profile Discovery Boundaries

Research profiles help readers find work and reconcile identity. They require narrower claims than canonical repository records.

## What a profile route can establish

- A public page can establish that a destination currently exposes a route, name, identifier or displayed record.
- A signed-in read-back can establish that an account accepted a saved field.
- A canonical DOI record can establish deposited title, creators, date, version, type and declared licence.
- A dated search result can establish observed indexing at that time.

## What it cannot establish by itself

- Profile presence does not establish peer review, journal acceptance, independent citation, research quality or endorsement.
- A platform's publication count does not establish the number of distinct studies; versions, mirrors, registrations and imported records can raise the displayed count.
- A saved profile field does not establish anonymous availability or search indexing until each is checked separately.
- Membership in a research community does not mean that the community endorses the member's work.
- Linking ORCID does not guarantee that a destination imports, synchronizes or correctly groups every ORCID work.

## Safe verification sequence

1. Match the person using the canonical ORCID, exact name and source-backed affiliation.
2. Match each work by exact DOI, title, creator order, type, date and version.
3. Check whether another DOI is a version, registration or mirror of an existing work.
4. Save only supported corrections and retain destination receipts.
5. Reload the signed-in record, then independently open the public route.
6. Record saved, submitted, approved, public, search-indexed and independently cited as separate states.
