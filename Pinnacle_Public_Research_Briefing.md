# Developmental measurement, participation and research methods

## A reader's guide to the Koti Reddy Saripalli / BHCL research portfolio

**Source metadata checked: 22 September 2026. Publication links and portfolio reconciliation: 23 September 2026.** This guide connects seven canonical repository works: six preprints and one institutional framework report. It explains what each work asks, what it reports or proposes, and where a reader can inspect the source. It is a reading aid, not an additional study, a systematic review, clinical guidance or an independent assessment of the instruments.

Koti Reddy Saripalli is affiliated with **Bharath Healthcare Laboratories Private Limited (Pinnacle Blooms Network), India**. His [ORCID](https://orcid.org/0009-0007-7866-0737) identifies the author. The [research library](https://www.pinnacleblooms.org/verify/evidence/research-library.html) connects the original records, author credits and publication stages.

Completed journal peer review is not established for the seven versions covered here. Repository deposit, DOI assignment, software licensing and researcher-profile verification answer different questions; none independently establishes clinical validity or treatment effectiveness.

## Choose a starting point

| Reader's question | Start with | What is available |
|---|---|---|
| Where are rehabilitation-training institution entries concentrated in the selected Indian register? | **RCI register analysis**, work 2 | Reported descriptive findings, source-linked institution data and reproduction code |
| How should a developmental-support software licensing case be documented? | **Regulatory case study**, work 1 | A document-based case analysis and claim–evidence framework |
| How is AbilityScore described and what evidence is still needed? | **Development monograph**, work 5, followed by **PINNACLE-VAL**, work 4 | Instrument description and preliminary operational metrics, followed by a formal validation plan |
| How can routine follow-up records be analysed without selecting only children who return? | **PINNACLE-TRAJ**, work 3 | A baseline-defined retrospective cohort protocol and analysis plan; no cohort results |
| What does a change in participation mean to a child or caregiver? | **Participation review protocol**, work 7 | Planned methods for evaluating important-change estimates; no review results |
| What is the institution's wider framework and historical research narrative? | **PGAF report**, work 6 | An institutional framework report with distinct author, editor and research-group credits |

The three protocol works address different evidence gaps. PINNACLE-VAL concerns comparator validity, reliability and preliminary meaningful change; PINNACLE-TRAJ concerns score trajectories and follow-up in routine care; the participation review concerns how published important-change estimates should be interpreted. They are not three completed demonstrations of effectiveness.

## 1. Documentary evidence for developmental-support software regulation

**Exact title:** *Licensing Developmental-Support Software in India: An April 2026 Case Study Assessed Against CDSCO's July 2026 Guidance*

**Author:** Koti Reddy Saripalli. **Version:** 3.0. **Deposit date:** 15 September 2026. **Status:** analytical case-study preprint; not peer reviewed. **DOI:** [10.5281/zenodo.22761782](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22761782).

**Research question and approach.** How can an April 2026 Indian licensing case for PinnacleAI GPT-OS v1.0.0 be assessed against medical-device-software guidance released in July 2026? The report examines electronic licence documents, the accompanying letter, quality-system certificates, the manufacturer's account and public regulatory sources. The comparison is retrospective: the July guidance postdates the April case.

**What the source reports.** Its documentary review identifies an MD-5 licence and covering letter describing a Class B scope, an application dated 16 April and a grant dated 21 April 2026. Separately, it attributes a 13 April classification decision and an 18 April audit to the manufacturer's account. The report separates evidence of licensing, software release, quality management, technical verification, clinical evaluation and outcomes.

**Interpretation and limits.** The author did not independently validate digital signatures or current registry status. The result is an account of a documented pathway and an evidence framework. It does not establish effectiveness, authorisation in other countries, or the licensing time that another product would require. The author's commercial interests and source-verification limits are disclosed in the manuscript.

**Inspect or reuse.** The [Zenodo record](https://zenodo.org/records/22761782) provides the [PDF](https://zenodo.org/api/records/22761782/files/01_Regulatory_Case.pdf/content), editable manuscript and supplementary figures/captions. This work does not provide a participant dataset. Its useful methodological contribution is the separation of a claim from the particular document capable of supporting it.

## 2. Geographic concentration in a fixed rehabilitation-training register

**Exact title:** *Geographic Concentration of RCI-Listed Rehabilitation Training Institutions in India: A Reproducible National Register Analysis*

**Author:** Koti Reddy Saripalli. **Version:** 2.0. **Deposit date:** 15 September 2026. **Status:** descriptive register-analysis preprint; not peer reviewed. **DOI:** [10.5281/zenodo.22761810](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22761810).

**Research question and approach.** How are institutional entries distributed across India's states and union territories in the RCI register dated 4 February 2026? The analysis reconstructs institution codes and printed serial numbers from a fixed 113-page document, checks jurisdiction assignments and source labels, and retains all 36 jurisdictions, including those absent from the source.

**Reported findings.** The reconstruction contains 1,068 unique institution codes in 32 jurisdictions. Uttar Pradesh and Rajasthan together account for 482 entries, or 45.13%. The largest five jurisdictional counts contain 62.08% of entries and the largest ten contain 79.31%. The reported Gini coefficient across all 36 jurisdictions is 0.6914 and HHI is 0.1216. These values describe concentration of register entries.

**Interpretation and limits.** One code is not necessarily one campus, and one listing is not an annual seat, a currently approved course, an active institution or an available clinician. The study does not measure clinical quality, workforce shortage or population-adjusted need. A zero means absence from this source snapshot. The extraction was computational and AI-assisted with source reconciliation, not independent human double extraction.

**Inspect or reproduce.** The [versioned Zenodo record](https://zenodo.org/records/22761810) contains the manuscript and [data, code and figures package](https://zenodo.org/api/records/22761810/files/02_RCI_Institution_Geography_Data_Code_and_Figures.zip/content). The public repository also provides the [data dictionary and reproduction guide](https://github.com/drkotireddysaripalli/pinnacle-research-library/blob/main/RCI_DATA_README.md), [audited institution table](https://github.com/drkotireddysaripalli/pinnacle-research-library/blob/main/workforce_institutions_audited.csv), [source manifest](https://github.com/drkotireddysaripalli/pinnacle-research-library/blob/main/workforce_source_manifest.json) and [reproduction script](https://github.com/drkotireddysaripalli/pinnacle-research-library/blob/main/reproduce_workforce.py). The manifest identifies the official source and checksum. These are institutional register records, not private clinical records.

## 3. Score trajectories and missing follow-up in routine developmental care

**Exact title:** *Six-Month Change and Follow-up Patterns in AbilityScore During Routine Paediatric Developmental Care in India: Protocol for the PINNACLE-TRAJ Retrospective Multicentre Cohort Study*

**Author:** Koti Reddy Saripalli. **Version:** 1.1. **Deposit date:** 15 September 2026. **Status:** retrospective cohort protocol with statistical analysis plan; preprint; no study results. **DOI:** [10.5281/zenodo.22761832](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22761832).

**Research question and proposed design.** What is the mean six-month change in AbilityScore within an eligible routine-care cohort, and whose follow-up assessments are available? The proposed study includes children younger than 13 at their first eligible baseline without requiring a later visit for entry. A comparable scoring era and eligible centres are to be fixed from deployment and measurement records before outcome inspection.

The protocol proposes a 30 June 2026 database close, a primary assessment window of days 150–210, and the available comparable score nearest day 180. This gives a latest eligible baseline of 2 December 2025 under the proposed close. It plans multilevel multiple imputation under a stated missing-at-random assumption, centre-aware uncertainty and sensitivity analyses using observed cases, weighting and missing-not-at-random assumptions.

**Status and limits.** The deposited source reports ethics review, registration and final scoring-era selection as pending; cohort size is not yet established. Its design can describe change and reassessment in the eligible service population. Without a comparison design, it cannot isolate treatment effectiveness or an AI contribution, establish a clinically important difference, or supply population developmental norms.

**Inspect or reuse.** The [record](https://zenodo.org/records/22761832) includes the [protocol PDF](https://zenodo.org/api/records/22761832/files/03_PINNACLE_TRAJ_Protocol.pdf/content), editable manuscript, blank reporting templates and design figures. The blank materials are planning resources, not observed participant data or completed analyses. No published cohort dataset is established by this deposit.

## 4. Formal AbilityScore psychometric validation: the protocol and later registration status

**Exact title:** *Concurrent Validity, Test-Retest Reliability, and Preliminary Minimal Clinically Important Difference of PinnacleAI AbilityScore: A Cross-Sectional Psychometric Validation Study Protocol*

**Authors, in order:** Koti Reddy Saripalli; Raghupathi Ramtenki; Srikanth Yadav Boini. **Version:** 2.1. **Deposit date:** 9 April 2026; the PDF is internally dated March 2026. **Status:** psychometric validation protocol preprint, not a validation-results paper. **DOI:** [10.5281/zenodo.19482476](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19482476). The PDF identifies Koti Reddy Saripalli as **Co-Investigator (Sponsor)** and corresponding author.

**Research question and proposed design.** How does AbilityScore relate to established comparator instruments, how stable is it across a short retest interval, and what preliminary meaningful-change estimate can be examined? The plan is a multicentre cross-sectional study with a nested prospective retest substudy. It proposes **300 children** aged seven months to seven years across six centres, with **60 of those children** reassessed after seven days. These are targets, not verified achieved samples. Comparator instruments include Vineland-3, CARS-2 and age-appropriate Bayley-4 or ABAS-3. Comparator raters and AbilityScore raters are to be blinded to each other's scores.

**What the plan can and cannot establish.** The intended analyses address correlations, agreement, reliability and a preliminary important-change estimate. The change anchor is the **child's primary therapist's** Global Impression of Change; it is not a child- or caregiver-reported anchor. The source itself distinguishes convergent validity from interchangeability and treats the cross-sectional anchor estimate as preliminary. This protocol does not supply completed validation results or a definitive individual decision threshold.

**Registration reconciliation, verified 23 September 2026.** The April abstract mentions a preregistered OSF analysis plan, while the PDF also says registration is still to be completed. The later record must not be described as prospective preregistration: the author has confirmed that some planned analyses had already run. The **retrospective** registration has now completed approval on OSF and is marked public, with DOI [10.17605/OSF.IO/78WF5](https://doi.org/10.17605/OSF.IO/78WF5), under CC BY 4.0. Actual achieved sample, analysis dates/results and current ethics/CTRI identifiers remain unresolved. This update changes how the study's transparency history should be described; it does not turn the protocol into a results paper. [OSF 78wf5](https://osf.io/78wf5/) is a companion registration to this work, not an eighth independent study.

**Inspect or reuse.** Read the original PDF through the [Zenodo record](https://zenodo.org/records/19482476). It proposes controlled data access after manuscript publication, subject to ethics approval and data-use agreements. No open participant dataset or completed analysis package has been verified. The source's ICC notation and absolute-agreement wording require reconciliation when the actual analysis is reported; they should not be silently rewritten in a summary.

## 5. AbilityScore architecture and preliminary operational evidence

**Exact title:** *AbilityScore: Development and Preliminary Psychometric Characterization of a Standardized Composite Index for Quantifying Child Developmental Readiness*

**Authors, in order:** Koti Reddy Saripalli; Raghupathi Ramtenki; Srikanth Yadav Boini. **Version:** **Technical Monograph v3.0 on the PDF cover**. **Deposit date:** 9 April 2026; PDF internally dated March 2026. **Status:** methodology and preliminary psychometrics preprint. **DOI:** [10.5281/zenodo.19482123](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19482123). The current Zenodo API has no version value; v3.0 is a PDF-derived bibliographic fact, not a filled-in API field.

**Research question and approach.** How is the composite index constructed, and what initial evidence is reported from routine operations? The monograph describes structured observations, a hierarchy of skills and abilities, a weighted composite and domain-specific readiness indexes. The described architecture combines 339 skills and 148 abilities, with seven readiness indexes. These are design features reported by the authors, not independently established measurement properties.

**Reported preliminary evidence.** The monograph reports operational inter-rater correlation **r=0.91** and **ICC=0.88 (95% CI 0.84–0.91)**, alongside MAPE and a bias variance ratio. Those operational metrics are not results from the planned PINNACLE-VAL comparator study. The document's abstract and dataset-provenance section give different session totals; this guide does not merge them into an achieved validation sample or treat all service encounters as independent research participants.

**Interpretation and limits.** The source acknowledges missing formal concurrent validity, controlled test-retest evidence and a formally established important-change threshold. It also states that AbilityScore has not yet been directly administered to a dedicated typically developing normative cohort. Parameter portability to other health systems is not assumed. The work is funded by BHCL and discloses the lead author's company role. Its value for a reader is a documented instrument architecture and an explicit programme of validation needs.

**Inspect or reuse.** The [source record](https://zenodo.org/records/19482123) provides the [full monograph PDF](https://zenodo.org/api/records/19482123/files/PinnacleAI%20AbilityScore%20Standardized%20Composite%20Index%20for%20Quantifying%20Child%20Developmental%20Readiness.pdf/content). Aggregate metrics are described in the manuscript; individual-level data are not publicly available. The source makes research-data requests conditional on ethics approval and data-use agreements. Publication of this manuscript does not establish public release of the production scoring system or its calibration data.

## 6. The PGAF institutional framework report

**Exact title:** *Pinnacle Global Autism Framework (PGAF): India's First Validated, Scalable, Outcome-Based Child Development System*

**Creator/author:** Koti Reddy Saripalli. **Editor:** Sreeja Reddy Saripalli. **Research-group contributor:** Koti Group SEVA Foundation. **Version:** v1.0. **Deposit date:** 22 May 2025. **Status:** institutional framework report. **DOI:** [10.5281/zenodo.15487405](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.15487405).

**Purpose and contents.** This report brings together the institution's developmental framework and company-study narrative around AbilityScore, TherapeuticAI, SEVA, Everyday Therapy and TherapySphere. It is a route to the framework's historical description and associated study summaries.

**Interpretation and limits.** The deposited title is reproduced exactly for identification. Its priority and validation language is not an independently verified conclusion of this guide. A report containing several study descriptions is one report in this seven-work bibliography; the descriptions are not automatically separate peer-reviewed publications or interchangeable samples. Patent references do not establish granted status, and citations of public bodies do not imply their endorsement. Preserve Sreeja Reddy Saripalli's editor credit and the Foundation's research-group credit rather than converting either into an author.

**Inspect.** The [Zenodo report record](https://zenodo.org/records/15487405) supplies the original whitebook PDF and structured contributor roles. No separately identified, openly downloadable participant dataset or report-wide reproduction code is verified here. Readers assessing a specific result should follow that result to its actual methods, denominator, period and supporting record rather than infer validation from the framework title.

## 7. Meaningful change in childhood participation

**Exact title:** *Meaningful Change in Childhood Participation: A Systematic Review Protocol for Child- and Caregiver-Anchored Estimates in Neurodevelopmental Disability*

**Authors, in order:** Koti Reddy Saripalli; Raghupathi Ramtenki; Srikanth Yadav Boini. **Version:** 1.0. **Deposit date:** 18 September 2026. **Status:** unregistered systematic-review protocol preprint; not peer reviewed; no review findings. **DOI:** [10.5281/zenodo.22827813](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22827813).

**Research question and proposed design.** Which child- or caregiver-anchored estimates of important change in participation have been reported, what do they mean, and how credible are they? The plan concerns longitudinal studies of children younger than 18 at baseline with specified neurodevelopmental conditions. Attendance and involvement are distinguished. The unit of extraction is an estimate or a documented failed attempt, linked to its study family.

Two human reviewers are planned for study selection, extraction and appraisal. The proposed outputs are an estimate catalogue and a map of evidence gaps and credibility. Individual responder thresholds, average changes in anchor-defined groups and longitudinal group contrasts retain different interpretations. Supporting measurement-property appraisal draws on applicable COSMIN methods. Narrative synthesis is planned; meta-analysis is not.

**Status and limits.** Exploratory scoping and reference checks have begun, but the deposited source reports that formal eligible-study searches, screening, extraction, appraisal and synthesis have not. PROSPERO submission is planned subject to eligibility and declarations; no registration number is assigned. Search access, specialist review, author-specific declarations, adjudication arrangements and full comparison with two recent predecessor reviews remain incomplete. No first-review claim is made. This work does not validate AbilityScore or establish treatment effectiveness.

**Inspect or reuse.** The [record](https://zenodo.org/records/22827813) provides a [reader edition](https://zenodo.org/api/records/22827813/files/P15_v1_Reader_Edition.pdf/content), [Supplementary Methods](https://zenodo.org/api/records/22827813/files/P15_v1_Supplementary_Methods.pdf/content), editable manuscript and a [complete package](https://zenodo.org/api/records/22827813/files/P15_Publication_v1_2026-09-18.zip/content) containing draft searches, worksheets and header-only execution forms. Worked examples are synthetic. These resources contain neither completed review findings nor patient-level data. The source discloses BHCL's staff-time/administrative support and the lead author's equity and relevant intellectual-property interests.

## Using the portfolio accurately

Use the DOI of the source actually read and preserve its complete author order, date, version and output type. The repository metadata identifies all seven records as CC BY 4.0; source attribution and any separately identified third-party rights still matter. Public access to an article does not imply open access to participant data, clinical assessment materials, production software or proprietary calibration parameters. The public code/data links above refer specifically to the RCI register analysis.

A productive reading sequence for measurement researchers is **development monograph → validation protocol → longitudinal protocol → participation review protocol**. This connects instrument definition, measurement evaluation, longitudinal observation and interpretation of meaningful change while keeping their different research questions visible. For data-methods readers, start with the RCI source manifest, audited table and reproduction guide. For regulatory readers, start with the document classes and verification limits in the licensing case study.

The parent handbooks, company-study summaries, patents and profile pages linked from the wider library have their own roles and attribution. They are not added to this seven-work research count. This guide should be revised when actual study results, approved/public registrations or corrected source versions become available; until then, the dates and limits above belong with the work descriptions.
