# Pinnacle’s public research library

## Six publications on developmental measurement, care and research

**Research briefing | 15 September 2026**

Families, clinicians and researchers need to know what a developmental measure records, how consistently it works and what a change in score means in everyday life. Pinnacle’s public research library brings together six works addressing parts of that question, alongside software regulation and rehabilitation training institutions in India.

**Current publication status: five preprints and one report.** These are openly available documents; this briefing does not present them as peer-reviewed journal articles. A study protocol describes planned research, while a completed analysis reports findings from the sources it examined. Each work below is identified by its permanent DOI.

### 1. A documented regulatory pathway for developmental-support software

**Preprint v3.0, 15 September 2026. Author: Koti Reddy Saripalli.**

This case study examines electronic copies of the April 2026 manufacturing licence and related documents for PinnacleAI GPT-OS v1.0.0, then assesses the case against CDSCO’s July 2026 software guidance. It explains the documented Class B scope for non-diagnostic developmental-support software and distinguishes licensing, quality systems, software testing and clinical evaluation. **What it establishes:** an account of the inspected documents and a framework for matching regulatory claims to evidence. **What it does not establish:** treatment effectiveness, independent validation of digital signatures or current registry status, or authorization to market the software in other countries. [Read the regulatory case study](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22761782).

### 2. Where RCI-listed rehabilitation training institutions are located

**Preprint v2.0, 15 September 2026. Author: Koti Reddy Saripalli.**

This completed descriptive analysis reconstructs **1,068 distinct institution codes** from the Rehabilitation Council of India list dated **4 February 2026**. It includes all **36 states and union territories**, with entries present in 32. Uttar Pradesh and Rajasthan together account for **482 entries, or 45.13%**. The accompanying data, source references and code allow others to examine the reconstruction. **What it establishes:** the geographic concentration of entries in that specific register snapshot. **What it does not establish:** how many institutions are currently operating, how many students or practitioners they supply, or whether a region has enough services for its population. [Read the national register analysis](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22761810).

### 3. PINNACLE-TRAJ: a plan to study six-month score change and follow-up

**Preprint v1.1, 15 September 2026. Author: Koti Reddy Saripalli.**

PINNACLE-TRAJ sets out a retrospective study of routine developmental-care records. Children would enter the study at an eligible baseline assessment and remain in its reporting even if they do not return. The plan defines a six-month assessment window, requires comparable scoring versions and specifies how missing follow-up would be analysed. **What it establishes:** a proposed analysis and reporting plan. **What remains pending in the released protocol:** ethics and waiver decisions, registration, the eligible scoring era and cohort size. It reports no study results. Its uncontrolled design cannot establish that therapy caused score changes, isolate an AI effect or prove school readiness or independence. [Read the PINNACLE-TRAJ protocol](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.22761832).

### 4. How AbilityScore is constructed

**Preprint, deposited 9 April 2026. Repository version v1; manuscript labelled Technical Monograph v3.0. Authors: Koti Reddy Saripalli, Raghupathi Ramtenki and Srikanth Yadav Boini.**

The AbilityScore monograph describes a constructed **0–1000 developmental index** that combines structured observations of skills and abilities, with domain profiles and adjustment for age-related expectations. The authors report preliminary scoring-consistency estimates from routine operations and explain the instrument’s intended role in tracking recorded development. **What it provides:** a description of the proposed measurement architecture and author-reported operational evidence. **What it does not yet establish:** completed formal validation against established measures, controlled short-interval test–retest reliability, directly collected population norms or a validated threshold for meaningful improvement. Consistent scores alone do not establish what the scores accurately measure. [Read the AbilityScore monograph](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19482123).

### 5. PINNACLE-VAL: a plan to test the measurement

**Preprint v2.1, deposited 9 April 2026. Authors: Koti Reddy Saripalli, Raghupathi Ramtenki and Srikanth Yadav Boini.**

This validation protocol proposes comparing AbilityScore with established instruments, including Vineland-3, CARS-2, Bayley-4 and ABAS-3. Its planned main sample is 300 children, with a 60-child subgroup assessed again after seven days. It also seeks to estimate a preliminary threshold for clinically meaningful score change. **What it establishes:** the questions and proposed methods for testing agreement with other measures and score stability. **What it does not establish:** that those tests have been completed or passed, that recruitment targets were reached, or that an improvement threshold is validated. The deposited protocol contains no assigned CTRI number and describes registration steps as pending. [Read the PINNACLE-VAL protocol](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19482476).

### 6. The Pinnacle Global Autism Framework report

**Report v1.0, 22 May 2025. Author: Koti Reddy Saripalli. Repository contributor credits: Sreeja Reddy Saripalli, editor; Koti Group SEVA Foundation, research group.**

The PGAF whitebook describes a connected service model bringing together AbilityScore measurement, TherapeuticAI planning support, parent-guided Everyday Therapy, the SEVA access model and TherapySphere environments. It summarizes author-reported studies and presents ideas for families, services and public programmes. **What it provides:** an account of the framework’s components, priorities and earlier reported experience. **What it does not independently establish:** causal benefits of the full programme or its components, successful implementation in other health systems, or readiness for population-wide adoption. Its broad validation claims should be assessed through the underlying methods, data and independent research, rather than inferred from the report’s availability. [Read the PGAF report](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.15487405).

## What the evidence means today

The library makes the research questions and available documents easier to inspect. It includes a reproducible analysis of a public institutional register, a documentary regulatory case, a measurement description, two planned studies and a broader framework report. These contributions answer different questions. Further measurement validation, documented scoring comparability and completed research with appropriate safeguards are needed to support stronger conclusions about children’s outcomes. Families’ confidence deserves evidence that is clear about both its findings and its limits.
